Guidance note 13

After Your Permit Is Issued

The permit isn't the finish line. Here's what keeps the approval valid through the build and after it.
During the Build
  • Build what was approved. The permit covers the stamped plans and nothing else. Keep a copy on site, and check the permit for conditions — some councils add their own.
  • Change of plan? Stop and amend. Moved it, enlarged it, swapped the engineering — lodge an amendment with us (the portal's Amend page) and a BA19 with the council before the change is built. Amending on paper is quick; retrofitting an approval around as-built work is not.
  • Watch the clock. A building permit has a limited life (commonly two years — the date is printed on it). If the build won't finish in time, apply to the council to extend before it lapses.
When the Work Is Finished
  • Give the council a notice of completion (BA13) within 7 days of finishing — it's the builder's obligation under the Building Act, and the form is on our Resources page.
  • If the permit carries conditions — retaining certification, an engineer's inspection, final details — close them out and keep the paperwork.
  • Pools and spas: the barrier obligation is permanent. Your local government inspects on an ongoing cycle (at least every four years in WA) — see note 09.
Keep Your Records

Save the whole package — certificate, stamped plans, engineering, permit and completion notice. It's what a future buyer's settlement agent, an insurer, or the council will ask for, years after everyone's memory has faded. Your issued documents stay downloadable in the portal for three months after first download, then they're removed — so file them with the job, not "later".

The call we get most: "the build changed a bit — is that a problem?" It's only a problem if the paperwork never catches up. Ring us the day the change is contemplated and it's usually a same-week fix.
