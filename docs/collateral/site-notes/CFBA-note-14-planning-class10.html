Guidance note 14

Planning approval & your Class 10 structure

Why a perfect certificate can still stall at the council — and how to know before you lodge.
Two approvals, not one

The building permit (what our certificate supports) says the structure is safe and compliant. Development approval — "planning" — says it's allowed there: setbacks, streetscape, size. They're separate systems, and the council will not issue a building permit while a required planning approval is missing. It's the single most common reason a correctly certified Class 10 job stalls.

When planning approval is usually NOT needed
  • The structure sits behind the front building line and meets the R-Codes deemed-to-comply setbacks for its height and length.
  • It's an ordinary ancillary structure — patio, shed, carport — within the size your local planning scheme exempts. Each council sets its own thresholds, so check yours or ask us.
  • Boundary walls that fit within the R-Codes boundary-wall provisions for your area's R-coding — see note 08, Building on or near a boundary.
When it usually IS needed
  • Anything forward of the front setback — carports in front of the dwelling are the classic.
  • Setback variations — closer to a boundary, longer or taller on the boundary, than the deemed-to-comply tables allow.
  • Corner lots (sight lines), heritage areas, and estates with design guidelines.
  • Retaining and fill that changes ground levels near boundaries.
Every council reads this differently. The R-Codes are state-wide but each local planning scheme adds its own exemptions and rules. When it's marginal, ask the council's planning desk — every Perth council is linked under Resources → Your local government — or ask us and we'll tell you which way it will land.
What to tell us when you lodge
  • Whether development approval exists, is in progress, or isn't needed — say which in the lodgement notes.
  • A site plan with setbacks dimensioned to every boundary — it's how we (and the council) judge the planning question at a glance.
  • If a variation is involved: the approval, or the neighbour comment the council collected.
